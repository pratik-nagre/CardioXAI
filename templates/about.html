Thesis Overview

About This Project

This project represents a Major Project I submission investigating the application of Explainable AI and Counterfactual Recourse to the problem of cardiovascular risk reduction. The core premise is that simply predicting risk is insufficient — a clinically useful system must also generate actionable, feasible, and explainable pathways for reducing that risk.

Research Problem

Problem Statement

Cardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of death globally. While machine learning models can predict CHD risk with high accuracy, they are often criticized as "black boxes" that provide predictions without insight or actionability. A physician who knows a patient's 10-year risk is 35% gains limited value without a corresponding answer to: "What can we change to reduce this?"

"Given a patient's clinical profile and a trained ensemble risk model, generate the minimal, feasible, and physiologically constrained set of clinical interventions that would move the patient from a high-risk to a low-risk classification — and explain why each intervention reduces risk." — Core Research Question, CardioXAI

This project addresses this gap by combining counterfactual recourse from algorithmic fairness literature with survival analysis from clinical biostatistics, mediated by a VAE-based latent space that enforces physiological realism.

Dataset

The Framingham Heart Study

The Framingham Heart Study (FHS) is one of the longest-running and most influential cardiovascular epidemiological studies in history. Begun in 1948 under the direction of the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI), it enrolled residents of Framingham, Massachusetts, and has followed them (and subsequent generations) through repeated clinical examinations.

The dataset used in this project (frmgham2.csv) contains longitudinal examinations across multiple periods. This study uses only the first examination (PERIOD=1), treating it as a cross-sectional baseline snapshot to avoid temporal dependency complexity and ensure clean causal interpretation of features.

Literature Context

Related Work

This project builds upon several key research threads: algorithmic recourse / counterfactual explanation, survival analysis in clinical AI, and deep generative models for tabular data.

2017 · Wachter et al.

Counterfactual Explanations Without Opening the Black Box

Seminal work proposing counterfactual explanations as a GDPR-compliant alternative to model interpretability. Introduced the "closest counterfactual" framing.

2020 · Pawelczyk et al.

Learning Model-Agnostic Counterfactual Explanations

Proposed REVISE and similar methods using VAE latent space search for plausible counterfactual generation — a direct inspiration for this project's approach.

2020 · Karimi et al.

Model-Agnostic Counterfactual Explanations for Consequential Decisions

Introduced the three-class constraint taxonomy (immutable, directional, actionable) that is central to the feasibility framework in this project.

2022 · Verma et al.

Counterfactual Explanations and Algorithmic Recourse

Comprehensive survey of recourse methods, highlighting the gap between proximity-optimized counterfactuals and clinically actionable interventions.

1972 · Cox, D.R.

Regression Models and Life-Tables

The foundational Cox Proportional Hazards model — still the gold standard for survival analysis in clinical research. Used here for its interpretable coefficients.

2013 · Kingma & Welling

Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes

The VAE paper. The reparameterization trick enables differentiable sampling from the latent space, making gradient-based latent optimization tractable.

Research Contributions

Key Contributions

  • 🧩

    Integrated Survival + Probabilistic Risk Framework

    Unlike most counterfactual systems that optimize a single classifier output, this system combines Cox log-hazard (survival model) with three tree-based classifiers (XGB, LGB, RF) into a unified risk objective. This provides more robust and clinically grounded optimization targets.

  • 🗺️

    Feasibility-Guided VAE Latent Space Search

    By searching within the VAE's latent manifold rather than directly in feature space, the system ensures counterfactuals are consistent with the overall patient distribution — eliminating physiologically impossible feature combinations.

  • ⚕️

    Clinical-Grade Constraint Taxonomy

    Formal implementation of immutable, directional, and actionable feature constraints as hard projection steps within the optimization loop. This ensures all recourse paths are medically and ethically valid.

  • 🔬

    Interpretable Feature Contribution Visualization

    Patient-level XAI via exact Cox coefficient decomposition — no approximation (unlike SHAP or LIME). Provides additive, directional risk attribution that is directly legible to clinicians.

  • 🖥️

    End-to-End Clinical Decision Support Interface

    A full-stack web application (FastAPI backend + interactive HTML/JS frontend) that enables physicians to load patient profiles, compute risk, and receive an AI-generated intervention prescription in real-time.

Future Directions

Limitations & Future Work

This project establishes a foundational framework with several clear avenues for future research and improvement:

🔄 SHAP Integration

Augment Cox coefficient contributions with SHAP values from the tree ensemble models for richer, non-linear explainability across all model types.

🏥 EHR Integration

Extend to real Electronic Health Record data formats (HL7 FHIR). Validate system performance on external cardiovascular cohorts (SCORE2, MESA).

🧬 Multi-Outcome

Expand prediction to multiple competing cardiovascular endpoints: stroke, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, in addition to CHD.

⏱️ Longitudinal Modeling

Incorporate multiple FHS examination periods to model intervention trajectories over time rather than static cross-sectional counterfactuals.

🤝 Clinician Validation

Conduct a structured user study with cardiologists to evaluate clinical utility, trust, and actionability of the AI-generated prescriptions.

📱 Mobile Interface

Develop a responsive mobile interface for point-of-care use in clinical settings, with offline inference capability for low-connectivity environments.
